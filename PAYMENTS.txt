GALEBREAK — SELLING SPARKS
==========================

The code is finished and the money never touches this game. The browser asks
your server for a Stripe Checkout link, Stripe hosts the card form on their
own page, and Stripe calls your server back afterwards. Sparks are credited
only from that signed callback, never from anything the player's browser
claims. A forged callback is rejected, and a repeated one cannot pay twice.

TURNING IT ON
  Three environment variables on your host:

      STRIPE_SECRET          sk_live_...  or sk_test_... while testing
      STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET  whsec_...
      PUBLIC_URL             https://your-game-address

  Then in the Stripe dashboard: Developers -> Webhooks -> add an endpoint at

      https://your-game-address/api/stripe-webhook

  and subscribe it to  checkout.session.completed.  Stripe shows you the
  signing secret once; that is STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET.

  Use the test keys first. Stripe's test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 with any
  future expiry and any CVC completes a purchase without real money moving.

  NEVER put STRIPE_SECRET in index.html, in the repo, or anywhere a player
  can read it. It is the key to your money. Environment variables only.

PRICES
  Edit SPARK_PACKS near the top of server.js. Amounts are in cents, so 99
  means $0.99. Set CURRENCY if you are not selling in dollars.


BEFORE YOU SWITCH IT ON — THINGS CODE CANNOT SOLVE

  You have to be old enough to hold the account.
    Stripe requires account holders to be 18. If you are not, this has to be
    a parent or guardian's account, in their name, with their bank details,
    and legally it is their business taking the money and their liability.
    There is no way around this and trying to work around it will get the
    account frozen with the balance in it.

  Your players may be children.
    Most of the audience for a Fortnite-like browser game is under 18. Many
    countries regulate selling virtual currency to minors: clear pricing in
    real money, no pressure tactics, and refunds when a child spends a
    parent's card without permission. Expect that last one to happen and
    decide now that you will simply refund it.

  Chargebacks are not optional.
    If someone disputes a payment, Stripe takes the money back plus a fee,
    whether or not you still have it. Keep that in mind before treating
    revenue as spent.

  Tax exists.
    Money you take is income and most places want it declared. Digital goods
    also attract VAT or sales tax in many countries. Stripe Tax can work this
    out automatically and is worth enabling.

  You need a refund policy and terms, in writing, on the page.
    Not legal cover for its own sake. It is what you point at when someone
    asks, and most payment processors require it.

  A quieter option worth considering
    Cosmetics in this game are already earned by playing. You could ask for
    support instead of selling currency — Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, YouTube
    memberships — and give supporters a cosmetic through the code system that
    already exists. No chargebacks, no tax on virtual goods, no rules about
    selling to children, and about ten minutes of setup rather than a legal
    obligation. For a game at this stage that is usually the better trade.
